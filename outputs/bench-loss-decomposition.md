# Bench: where v8 loses spellF1 (external matches only)

Generated by `python -m bench.report` from `work/bench/results.json` and `work/bench/audit.json`. Diagnostic only: it selects nothing (`work/BENCH.md`). Predictions are out-of-fold from the v6-recipe model inside v8; truth is scored under the four EXPERIMENT-08 label conventions and every number below is a mean over matches and then over the four conventions unless a column says otherwise.

## Summary

v8's recipe loses most of its spellF1 in possession segmentation, not in frame accuracy. All numbers are out-of-fold on external matches; none comes from the competition match.

- **It finds too few possessions.** Per match it predicts 215 possessions on SkillCorner against 290 true, and about 235 against 358 on DFL. Its possessions are 13 to 15 s long on average; the true ones are 9 to 11 s. Only 56% (SkillCorner), 60% (DFL clean) and 52% (DFL gaps) of true possessions are matched.
- **The lost possessions are the short ones.** True possessions under 3 s are matched 11% to 17% of the time under the raw provider labels, those of 3 to 6 s 43% to 55%, those of 12 s or more 85% to 94%. Most lost ones are swallowed by a long, mostly correct prediction for the other team (50 per match on SkillCorner, 78 on DFL clean) or merged into a longer prediction of the same team (30 and 48).
- **How big that is depends on the label convention.** Resolving the merged spans is worth +0.29 (SkillCorner) and +0.36 (DFL clean) spellF1 under raw labels, but +0.11 and +0.10 under the 3 s controlled-possession rule. The published competition statistics (mean possession 14.2 s) are longer than any external convention gives, so the realistic size of this lever is the controlled3 figure or less.
- **Dead ball matters on real tracking and under gaps, not on clean data.** A true D mask is worth +0.081 on SkillCorner (17% of the gap) and +0.065 on DFL gaps (13%), and +0.007 on clean DFL. v8 misses 19 of 112 stoppages per SkillCorner match and invents 14.
- **Timing of boundaries is a small lever.** Snapping every boundary to the nearest true one within 1 s gains +0.007 to +0.018; within 2 s, +0.024 to +0.039. Matched possessions start with a median error of 0.0 to 0.2 s after a stoppage and -0.5 to +0.2 s at a turnover.
- **Team assignment inside our own possessions is a medium lever** (+0.068 SkillCorner, +0.026 clean, +0.032 gaps for the true team on our possessions).
- **Ball gaps cost about 0.10.** On the simulated gaps, putting the true ball back where the simulation removed it gains +0.096 (19% of the gap).

## What the numbers do and do not say

- Oracle gains are upper bounds on the external truth, which is a provider convention. They overlap and cannot be added. The error-type rows write the truth over only the spans that carry one error, so they also bound a whole class at once ("every absorbed possession fixed"), not a model that could be trained.
- The competition truth is a different object: corrected tracking, with the public 5 minutes at 17 possessions, mean 14.2 s and D 19.6% of frames. No external source has a 5-minute window like that often (see the audit). The rows above are therefore best read for their ranking, with the controlled3 column as the more relevant magnitude.
- Gains were positive on every match for the main levers (20 of 20 SkillCorner matches, 7 of 7 DFL), so the ranking is not driven by one match.
- Not checked: any competition-side number other than the three published ones; whether a model trained on a longer-possession convention scores better under the competition convention (that needs the competition truth, which this track must not touch).
- Setting 3b (gaps plus the simulated second camera, the v8 analogue) is not in these tables. Its views take about 20 minutes each on this loaded machine and were still computing when this was written. `python -m bench.oof` resumes them, then `python -m bench.run && python -m bench.report` adds the column. Nothing else depends on it.

## Label audit in short (tables in section 6)

- **D (dead ball) is a long-stoppage label everywhere.** 90% to 95% of D frames sit in runs of 10 s or more in every source, and the D share of a match is 45% to 47% for DFL and SkillCorner (32% for the Metrica event proxy, which only marks ball-out and fault events). The public 5 minutes have 19.6% D. That value is reached by 4% of the 5-minute windows in DFL and SkillCorner and by 17% in Metrica. Either the public window is an unusually lively stretch or the competition marks dead ball less often than DFL's BallStatus. One window cannot say which, so nothing was chosen from it.
- **Possession length is where conventions differ.** Mean possession: DFL provider 7.3 s (43% under 3 s), controlled 1 s / 2 s / 3 s 8.3 / 9.8 / 11.3 s, SkillCorner group 9.6 s, SkillCorner phases of play 12.5 s, Metrica event-derived 11.3 s. The published mean is 14.2 s. The share of 5-minute windows with a mean of 14.2 s or more is 5% for the provider labels, 25% for controlled 3 s, 32% for SkillCorner phases and 15% for Metrica.
- **Deflections and contested balls.** The DFL provider possession flag changes on touches: it flips 278 to 408 times per match, which is 9% to 62% (mean 35%) above the number of turnover-type events in the DFL event files (tackles with possession change, ball claims, unsuccessful plays). A 1 s flip between two phases of the other team (A-h-A) occurs 1.6 times per 5 minutes in the provider labels, 0 after the 1 s rule. The Metrica proxy gives a possession to the passer or recoverer and ignores challenges and lost balls, so contested balls stay with the last team; but it counts every interception as a change at once. SkillCorner phases of play are the smoothest source (2% of possessions under 1 s).
- **Closest to the competition definition** (D where tracking marks the ball dead, otherwise the owning team, with deflections and uncontrolled touches not counting): DFL tracking BallStatus for D, and for possession the longest controlled rule (3 s) or SkillCorner phases of play. By the two published numbers alone the Metrica event proxy is nearest on D share and SkillCorner phases on mean length, but the proxy's D is not tracking-based. None of the sources reaches a 14.2 s mean, so a model trained on any of them is trained on shorter possessions than the competition shows.
